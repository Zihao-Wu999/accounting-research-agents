# Related Literature: Open scan of financial reporting, disclosure, auditing, and tax research opportunities; information cutoff October 20, 2026; news window April 20–October 20, 2026.

Recognition and disclosure. A first literature stream examines how reporting location and verifiability influence accounting judgments. Clor-Proell and Maines (2014) find that public-company preparers exert more effort and exhibit less strategic bias when contingent liabilities are recognized rather than disclosed. Bertomeu and Magee (2014) model the endogenous selection of asymmetric disclosure rules, emphasizing measurement informativeness and verifiability. These studies motivate examining tariff refunds as a sequence of disclosure, recognition, and collection. Their applicability is conceptual rather than direct: a recovery claim is not a contingent liability, and differences in recognition can reflect differences in underlying rights and evidence.

Tax transparency. Published research distinguishes additional information from strategic disclosure responses. Kays (2021) shows that firms supplement mandated Australian tax disclosures when reputational incentives favor explanation. Bilicka et al. (2025) find increased but more boilerplate disclosure following the UK tax-strategy mandate, without a significant tax-planning response. Burd (2026) associates numeric tax detail, especially footnote numbers, with improved implied ETR forecasts. Together, these findings caution against equating longer narratives with greater transparency. The ASU 2023-09 frontier is already crowded. Christensen et al. (2025) document favorable anticipatory market responses and reductions in disclosed tax risk attributable to reporting discretion. Wilde et al. (2026) examine first compliant filings and find positive return associations concentrated in rate-lowering tax-haven disclosures. Consequently, a new project must move beyond adoption effects toward residual aggregation and the interpretability of relationships between tax expense and cash payments. ([papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5542138&utm_source=openai))

Audit oversight. DeFond and Lennox (2017) connect inspection deficiencies to subsequent adverse internal-control opinions and higher fees, consistent with costly remediation. Nagy (2014) finds market-share losses following public quality-control criticism. These studies establish distinct remediation and reputational channels, but neither retrieved abstract answers whether system-level compliance costs change small auditors' participation in issuer auditing. QC 1000 offers a prospective setting for this question, although implementation outcomes cannot be studied as realized events at the October 20, 2026 cutoff. Any design must distinguish scale-related exposure from genuinely exogenous variation. ([onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1475-679X.12151?utm_source=openai))

Climate reporting. Ilhan et al. (2023) document institutional demand for climate-risk disclosure, whereas Downar et al. (2021) identify emissions reductions following a UK reporting mandate without a significant gross-margin effect. More directly, Bochkay et al. (2026) show that U.S. firms reduce salient ESG terminology and framework affiliations before broader disclosure substance weakens. This frontier sharply limits novelty for vocabulary-based retrenchment studies. A narrower extension would establish whether quantified climate information migrates into accounting estimates, expenditure discussion, and footnotes, while acknowledging that the May 2026 SEC action was a rescission proposal. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-021-09611-x))

Reporting frequency. Balakrishnan and Ertan (2017) link quarterly bank reporting to better loan quality, while Frankel et al. (2021) support complementarity between audited reporting and voluntary forecasts. These results suggest separating a reporting cadence from the information package delivered at that cadence. However, Islam and Wheatley (2025) and Fried (2026) have directly overlapping working-paper titles, and their missing abstracts prevent definitive novelty assessment. The feasible initial question concerns explicit commitments to preserve quarterly information after the May 5 proposal; actual switches must not be assumed. Across all streams, the most defensible opportunities combine validated accounting measures with restrained causal claims, rather than relying on a recent event alone.
