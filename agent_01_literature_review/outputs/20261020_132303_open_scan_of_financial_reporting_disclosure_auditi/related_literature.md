# Related Literature: Open scan of financial reporting, disclosure, auditing, and tax opportunities; news window April 20–October 20, 2026; literature window 2000–2026.

The reviewed literature suggests that recent accounting developments are most useful when they identify a specific measurement or verification boundary rather than merely a new disclosure topic. The five proposed studies therefore examine the composition of reported cash, the presentation of indirect-customer payments, the realization of cyber losses, the substantiation of clean-fuel benefits, and the valuation of servicing recapture. They are new relative to the supplied idea bank, but their novelty assessments remain conditional on incomplete frontier coverage.

Information quality and disaggregation provide the first stream. Lambert, Leuz, and Verrecchia (2007) show theoretically that accounting information can affect assessed cash-flow covariances and real decisions. Lang and Maffett (2011) document that transparency is associated with lower liquidity uncertainty, especially during crises. These studies motivate examining whether the composition of corporate cash equivalents supplies information beyond the aggregate balance. They do not establish that every additional component disclosure improves decisions. The proposed reconciliation measure therefore distinguishes quantified economic content from disclosure length. ([onlinelibrary.wiley.com](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1475-679X.2007.00238.x?utm_source=openai)) Adjacent frontier work includes Ballis et al. (2026) on stablecoin reserve disclosures and Huang, Korczak, and Tsileponis (2026) on corporate crypto holdings; the latter's findings were unavailable from the conference program. ([aaahq.org](https://aaahq.org/Meetings/2026/FARS-Midyear-Meeting/Program))

A second stream concerns discretion in revenue reporting. Du et al. (2026) find that principal-versus-agent exposure is associated with heightened compliance risk before ASC 606 and that these differences diminish afterward. Their evidence supports studying narrow contractual boundaries rather than treating revenue discretion as uniformly harmful. ([papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/SSRN_ID4111573_code371540.pdf?abstractid=4111573&utm_source=openai)) Myers et al. (2022) similarly examine the tension between useful discretion and opportunistic recognition. Payments to a customer's customer extend this discussion to presentation: reported revenue and expenses can differ even when the underlying transaction has similar operating-income consequences.

Cybersecurity research makes the strongest contribution to screening out superficially novel ideas. Lin and She (2025) identify suggestive evidence of strategic incident-discovery reporting. ([pubsonline.informs.org](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/abs/10.1287/mnsc.2023.01058?utm_source=openai)) Badger et al. (2026) directly examine materiality assessments and governance language in Item 1C disclosures. ([papers.ssrn.com](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=7397253&utm_source=openai)) Moreover, Badger, Binkley, and Arthur (2026) already present a paper on disclosure shifts following Item 1.05. Consequently, filing-channel substitution alone is not a defensible contribution. The proposed extension instead follows incident disclosures into recognized costs, recoveries, and audit outcomes, while explicitly avoiding hindsight-based judgments about initial materiality. ([aaahq.org](https://aaahq.org/Meetings/2026/AIS-SET/Program))

Tax information supplies a fourth stream. Bogachek et al. (2025) demonstrate that narratives distributed throughout the 10-K improve predictions of tax outcomes and reveal information that analysts do not fully incorporate. ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-025-09914-3?utm_source=openai)) This motivates a more granular investigation of whether technical substantiation predicts recognition and realization of Section 45Z benefits. Such a study must first establish each issuer's accounting policy rather than assume that all benefits affect the tax provision.

Finally, fair-value research cautions against equating managerial inputs with poor measurement. Altamuro and Zhang (2013) find that managerial-input MSR valuations better reflect underlying servicing-fee persistence and risk. ([repository.upenn.edu](https://repository.upenn.edu/bitstreams/bc4a8927-d559-4126-bdea-85f67a1f8851/download?utm_source=openai)) Fischer et al. (2026), however, show that reporting-risk and performance incentives affect revaluation timing. ([doi.org](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-026-09949-0?utm_source=openai)) Recapture offers a focused setting in which these informational and incentive explanations can be evaluated together. Across the agenda, proposals are not treated as enacted mandates, and future implementation designs remain conditional. Conference manuscripts, independent issuer counts, and WRDS subscriptions require verification before execution.
